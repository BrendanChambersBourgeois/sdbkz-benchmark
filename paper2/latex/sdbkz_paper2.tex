\begin{abstract}
The Root Hermite Factor (RHF) is the standard one-number summary of lattice-reduction
quality, and the companion paper showed on LWE-Kannan lattices that it is
structurally blind to a real difference between BKZ and self-dual BKZ
(SD-BKZ). This report measures what that blind spot hides on \emph{NTRU},
where it matters most: near the fatigue point, where reduction stops grinding
toward a shortest vector and instead discovers the dense secret sublattice.
We report three empirical results, each traceable to per-seed data.
First, the SD-BKZ advantage on circulant NTRU is not smooth in dimension: tied
with BKZ for $n\!\le\!67$, it spikes at $n\!\approx\!71$--$73$ (mean up to
$+9.7$ nats), then settles to a tight $+1.5$--$1.7$-nat plateau for $n\!\ge\!79$.
Second, in the overstretched $q$-onset regime the defensible NTRU observable is
\emph{reference-free} (there the signed $d(\mathrm{LN})$ becomes a reference
artifact, unlike on the $q=97$ sweep above), and under it SD-BKZ reaches
dense-sublattice discovery (DSD) at lower modulus than BKZ, the gap growing with
dimension at $\beta=20$ (${\approx}0\%$ at $n\!\le\!79$ to $28\%$ at $n\!=\!113$).
Third, the G6K sieve---a fundamentally different oracle---reproduces the thesis
and sharpens its scope: at matched $\beta=40$ the engines agree at $n=89$ (an
honest null) and the gap reopens at $n=101$ on both ($9\%$ fplll, $7\%$ G6K),
each engine's SD-BKZ firing on a near-superset of BKZ's; what differs between
engines is the sub-fatigue tail, the sieve clearing the shortest vector
${\approx}4.9\times$ longer than enumeration. All experiments are reproducible
via a containerised, determinism-gated benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Key terms.}
\begin{description}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item[\textnormal{\emph{NTRU lattice}}] a structured lattice hiding a short secret $(f,g)$; recovering it breaks the key.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{BKZ / SD-BKZ}}] block-reduction algorithms; SD-BKZ also reduces from the dual end each tour.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{Fatigue point}}] the modulus $q$ where the attack flips from solving SVP to finding the dense sublattice.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{DSD}}] dense-sublattice discovery---the reduced profile collapsing onto the secret sublattice.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{RHF}}] Root Hermite Factor: the usual one-number reduction-quality score---blind here.
\item[\textnormal{\emph{fplll / G6K}}] the two reduction engines: exact enumeration vs.\ randomised sieving.
\end{description}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

NTRU lattices fail differently from random ones. Past the fatigue
point~\cite{DucasWoerden2021}, reduction stops grinding toward a shortest
vector and instead discovers the dense secret sublattice wholesale---and where
that transition sits, and which reduction algorithm crosses it first, is
exactly the kind of question security estimation answers with models scored by
the Root Hermite Factor (RHF). The companion paper~\cite{Paper1} showed, on
LWE-Kannan lattices, that the RHF is structurally blind to differences between
BKZ and self-dual BKZ~\cite{EC:MicWal16} that the Li--Nguyen Rankin profile
distance $d(\mathrm{LN})$~\cite{EPRINT:LiNgu20} makes visible. This report
asks two follow-up questions a data paper is well suited to answer:
\emph{(i) does the SD-BKZ phenomenology generalise beyond LWE to NTRU?} and
\emph{(ii) does it reproduce on a fundamentally different reduction
oracle---sieving (G6K) rather than enumeration?} Both answers turn out to be
yes---each with a qualification the data forces, and one that only a
reference-free observable can see.

Our contributions are primarily empirical and infrastructural:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A characterisation of the SD-BKZ advantage on circulant NTRU
        lattices~\cite{DucasWoerden2021}, including a sharp dimension-onset
        transition (Section~\ref{sec:dimonset}).
  \item Evidence that the appropriate NTRU observable is reference-free, and a
        \emph{dense-sublattice-discovery (DSD) onset} whose SD-vs-BKZ gap grows
        with dimension as $q$ approaches the fatigue point
        (Section~\ref{sec:fatigue}).
  \item A cross-engine study (Section~\ref{sec:xengine}): G6K wired as a second
        engine behind a reproducibility-gated seam, a self-dual pump-and-jump
        construction validated against fplll's SD-BKZ, and confirmation that
        RHF-blindness is engine-independent but regime-dependent---consistent
        with independent observations of RHF-equality under a non-exact
        oracle~\cite{ABLR21,Rowell2024}.
  \item A criterion-sensitivity audit of the DSD flag itself: the loose
        $b_1$-only test over-fires on uniformly-reduced sub-fatigue bases,
        manufacturing an apparent cross-engine onset gap that the proper
        two-part criterion eliminates (Section~\ref{sec:xengine}); we flag it
        because it cost us a headline claim.
  \item An independent re-validation of an fplll GSO catastrophic-cancellation
        fix on a new (NTRU) modulus family (Appendix~\ref{sec:kahan}).
\end{itemize}

This is a \emph{technical report / data paper}: the emphasis is on the
experimental apparatus, the data, and reproducibility, rather than on new
theory. Closest prior work is Deng and Jia~\cite{DengJia2023}, who give a
sieving-based self-dual BKZ that puts a G6K Pump oracle in place of enumeration
and thereby attains comparable or better output quality (against the enumeration
baseline) at substantially lower running time, and who observe the SD-vs-BKZ
quality gap narrowing as the block size grows. They do not study NTRU or
dense-sublattice discovery, and their construction differs from ours---Pump
called per window inside the self-dual skeleton, versus our full pump-and-jump
tour followed by a dual tour. Our aim is complementary rather than
competing: we do not propose a faster oracle but a finer empirical
characterisation, measuring the SD-vs-BKZ structural difference through the
reference-free profile observable across a fixed-$\beta$, $q$-swept regime.
White~\cite{White26} reviews the Li--Nguyen dynamic-analysis framework and
identifies dual-lattice BKZ as a direction for it, pursued empirically here.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Circulant NTRU lattices and fatigue}
\label{sec:bg-ntru}
We use the circulant NTRU construction of Ducas and van
Woerden~\cite{DucasWoerden2021}. For a prime $n$ and modulus $q$, sample
ternary secrets $f,g\in\mathbb{Z}_q[x]/(x^n-1)$ (uniform $\{-1,0,1\}$,
coordinate variance $\sigma^2=2/3$) with $f$ invertible, set $h=g/f \bmod q$,
and form the $2n\times2n$ basis
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix} q I_n & 0 \\ H & I_n \end{pmatrix},
\]
where $H$ is the circulant matrix of $h$. The secret $(f,g)$ is an unusually
short vector in the row span; recovering it breaks the key. Two structures
distinguish $B$ from a random $q$-ary lattice: a \emph{$q$-vector head} (rows of
$qI_n$ that survive reduction as length-$q$ vectors) and a \emph{dense secret
sublattice} spanned by the rotations of $(f,g)$. A BKZ-reduced NTRU profile
therefore follows the \emph{ZGSA} (Z-shaped) model---a flat head at $\log q$,
then a GSA slope---rather than the single-slope GSA line of a strongly-reduced
random $q$-ary lattice. (Generic $q$-ary lattices carry a $q$-vector head too;
the NTRU-specific feature is the \emph{second} slope from the dense sublattice,
the geometry that~\cite{DEP2023} exploits.)

As $q$ grows at fixed $n$, the secret sublattice becomes relatively denser and
easier to detect: at the \emph{fatigue point}
$q_\mathrm{fat}(n)\approx0.004\,n^{2.484}$~\cite{DucasWoerden2021} (an empirical
fit for $n>100$, extrapolated below that range here, not a universal constant) the attack
transitions from the standard regime (secret found only through full SVP) to
the \emph{overstretched} regime, where reduction discovers the dense sublattice
(\emph{dense-sublattice discovery}, DSD) well before solving SVP. The geometry
of this transition---and how self-dual reduction moves through it---is the
object of this report.

\subsection{BKZ, SD-BKZ, and the two engines}
\label{sec:bg-bkz}
Block Korkine--Zolotarev (BKZ) reduction repeatedly calls an SVP oracle on
length-$\beta$ projected sublattice windows, sweeping left to right. Self-dual
BKZ (SD-BKZ)~\cite{EC:MicWal16} alternates a forward (primal) pass with a
backward pass on the reversed dual basis, so each tour reduces the basis from
both ends. On LWE-Kannan lattices the companion paper~\cite{Paper1} found the
two produce bases of indistinguishable Root Hermite Factor%
\footnote{We compare RHF \emph{differences} between BKZ and SD-BKZ on the same
lattice, which are independent of the $d$-vs-$(d{-}1)$ root-normalisation
convention.} yet measurably
different Rankin profiles; this report asks how that difference behaves on NTRU
and across two oracle implementations.

We use two engines. \textbf{fplll} supplies an exact enumeration SVP oracle;
its self-dual variant (\texttt{SD\_VARIANT}) is our ground truth. \textbf{G6K}~%
\cite{G6K2019} supplies an approximate \emph{sieving} oracle through the
pump-and-jump BKZ tour (\texttt{pump\_n\_jump\_bkz\_tour}); we realise SD-BKZ on
it as a primal pump-and-jump pass followed by a dual pass run under G6K's
\texttt{dual\_mode} (operations on the dual basis, bounds reflected about
$\mathrm{full\_n}/2$). The two oracles differ fundamentally---deterministic
enumeration versus randomised sieving---which is what makes a phenomenon that
survives both engines credible. Sieving carries a fixed setup cost that makes
small $\beta$ uninformative, so all cross-engine experiments use $\beta=40$,
the smallest block size at which the sieve does non-trivial work; this is also
the regime in which related $q$-ary forgery attacks operate~\cite{DEP2023}.

\subsection{The reference-free DSD observable}
\label{sec:bg-obs}
The companion paper's metric is $d(\mathrm{LN})=\frac1m\sum_i|r_i-\rho_i|$,
where $r_i$ is the slope-removed Gram--Schmidt log-norm profile and $\rho_i$ is
the Li--Nguyen BKZ fixed point~\cite{EPRINT:LiNgu20}---a GSA line valid for a
\emph{random $q$-ary} lattice. NTRU is not random $q$-ary (Section~%
\ref{sec:bg-ntru}): its reduced profile follows the ZGSA Z-shape, for which no
closed-form Li--Nguyen fixed point exists. Comparing an NTRU profile to the LWE
line therefore conflates structure with reduction dynamics, and the
\emph{signed} advantage $d(\mathrm{LN})_{\mathrm{BKZ}}-d(\mathrm{LN})_{\mathrm{SD}}$
can flip sign under a different reference---it is a \emph{reference artifact}.

Two observables survive this. First, the \emph{unsigned} profile divergence
$\frac1m\sum_i|r_i^{\mathrm{BKZ}}-r_i^{\mathrm{SD}}|$ is reference-free (it
never names $\rho$); on NTRU it is large precisely at the transition and
reproduces under three independent references. Second, and what we report
throughout, is a binary \emph{dense-sublattice-discovery} flag---a
\emph{two-part} criterion: a reduction has found the secret sublattice when
its profile has collapsed onto it,
$\#\{i:\log\|\mathbf b_i^\ast\| < \log\sqrt{2n\cdot2/3}+0.5\}\le n+1$,
\emph{and} the shortest GS vector clears the floor,
$b_1=\min_i\log\|\mathbf b_i^\ast\| > 1.5$. The count threshold is the
$q$-independent ternary-secret log-norm $\log\sqrt{2n\cdot2/3}$ ($=2.39$ at
$n=89$, which bounds from above the measured $q$-independent $b_1$ saturation
floor ${\approx}1.96$--$1.97$)
plus a $0.5$-nat margin, evaluated per $n$ ($2.75$ at $n=67$ to $3.01$ at
$n=113$; Section~\ref{sec:fatigue} reports the sensitivity to freezing it).
The $b_1>1.5$ half alone is necessary but not sufficient---it
over-fires on uniformly-reduced sub-fatigue bases
(Section~\ref{sec:xengine}). Both parts are properties of the output basis
alone, independent of any modelled reference. The onset $q$ is where the
per-seed DSD rate crosses $50\%$, linearly interpolated between adjacent
$q$-grid cells (\texttt{extract\_dsd\_onset.py}); the SD-vs-BKZ onset
\emph{gap} is the object of Sections~\ref{sec:fatigue} and~\ref{sec:xengine}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Generators and engines}
\label{sec:setup-engines}
NTRU bases are built by \texttt{generators/ntru.py} following Section~%
\ref{sec:bg-ntru}; each basis is checked per seed before any reduction
($\dim=2n$ and the key relation $H\!f\equiv g\pmod q$; the $qI_n$ block
layout is covered by unit tests).
Both engines sit behind a single seam: \texttt{run\_single} takes a
\texttt{backend} argument, and \texttt{make\_backend} returns either an fplll or
a G6K backend exposing a common \texttt{tour()}/\texttt{gso()} interface. The
per-tour reduction loop is then engine-blind---the same loop drives enumeration
and sieving, so the two cannot diverge through duplicated control flow. The G6K
self-dual tour is a primal \texttt{pump\_n\_jump\_bkz\_tour} followed by a dual
pass executed inside a \texttt{temp\_params} block that flips
\texttt{dual\_mode}; this was validated against fplll's \texttt{SD\_VARIANT}
on a random $q$-ary lattice ($n=80$, $\beta=40$, three tours) by checking
that the SD$-$BKZ profile-change signature (the GS-slope flattening) agrees
in direction and magnitude across engines (fplll $-0.00039$ vs G6K
$-0.00040$ per tour); its 50-tour NTRU behaviour is separately corroborated
by the cross-engine soundness check of Section~\ref{sec:xengine}.

\subsection{Reproducibility and determinism gates}
\label{sec:setup-repro}
Both engines run in containers built from a digest-pinned base with
\texttt{snapshot.debian.org} apt pinning; all source-built engine binaries
(G6K and the patched fplll) fix a \texttt{-march=x86-64-v3} target, and the
canonical fplll path ships the version-pinned \texttt{fpylll} 0.6.4
wheel---identical bytes on every machine. Each result is a
per-seed JSON carrying the full input parameters and output profile; a SHA-256
manifest gates byte-identity. The fplll path is held to numerical
reproducibility ($10^{-4}$ tolerance on reference seeds) by
\texttt{verify.sh} and to byte-identity by the SHA-256 seed manifest; the
G6K path to an exact SHA-256 match by \texttt{verify\_g6k.sh} against a
captured reference hash, confirmed on a second machine (the CI runner).

Determinism on the sieve required care (ADRs~005--008). G6K's multi-threaded
sieve has a nondeterministic reduction race, so all sieving runs single-threaded
($\texttt{threads}=1$); across-seed parallelism still uses all cores, since
seeds are independent. The fplll global RNG must be seeded both before basis
construction and again before the sieve (G6K samples from it), and the
\texttt{SieverParams} seed is a no-op that we never rely on. With these,
multi-tour G6K runs are bit-identical run-to-run, and---verified at $n=80$,
$\beta=60$, three tours---per-tour re-seeding is itself a no-op: the
construction seed alone determines all $N$ tours, so the policy is to seed once
at construction. The new modulus and engine introduced no schema change to the
seed JSON, preserving the companion paper's SHA baselines.

\subsection{Campaigns and parameter grids}
\label{sec:setup-campaigns}
Every experiment is a named campaign in \texttt{config/sweep.toml}, fixing
generator, engine, $n$-grid, $\beta$, $q$, tour count, and seed count so a
single command reproduces a cell. The dimension-onset sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:dimonset}) fixes $q=97$, $\beta=20$, 50 tours and sweeps
$n\in[59,89]$ at 20 seeds each ($n=71$ at 40). The fatigue and DSD-onset sweeps
(Section~\ref{sec:fatigue}) fix $n$ and sweep $q$ across the fatigue point. The
cross-engine sweeps (Section~\ref{sec:xengine}) fix $n=89$, $\beta=40$, 50 tours
and sweep a matched $q$-grid on \emph{both} engines---the G6K campaign
(\texttt{ntru\_g6k\_qsweep}) and the fplll campaign
(\texttt{ntru\_xeng\_qsweep}) write to leaves that share the same
$(q,\text{precision},\text{tours},n,\beta)$ key, so a comparator reads the two
engines cell-for-cell. The Kahan re-validation (Appendix~\ref{sec:kahan}) runs
$n=127$ at $q\in\{971,1087,1201\}$ through a separate patched-fplll campaign
whose seeds land in their own tree, never overwriting the canonical seeds.
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} summarises the full grid: 8{,}144 matched BKZ / SD-BKZ
reductions across $n\in[59,127]$, two block sizes, and 81 moduli, on both the
enumeration and sieve oracles.

\section{The SD-BKZ Advantage Has a Dimension-Onset on NTRU}
\label{sec:dimonset}

On circulant NTRU at $q=97$ ($\beta=20$, $50$ tours; standard regime
throughout the sweep, though only marginally so at the smallest dimension:
$q_\mathrm{fat}(59)\approx100$), the
SD-BKZ-vs-BKZ advantage has a \emph{sharp} dimension transition, not a smooth
ramp (20 seeds per $n$, $n=71$ topped to $40$):

Three regimes: tied for $n\!\le\!67$; an onset zone at $n=71$--$73$ where the
mean spikes to $+4$--$10$ nats, the onset-zone standard deviation
${\approx}27\times$ the plateau standard
deviation; and a stable $+1.5$--$1.7$-nat plateau for $n\!\ge\!79$ (std

magnitude above the LWE-Kannan scale (final $d(\mathrm{LN})$ ${\approx}4$--$5$

comparable within NTRU only---sign and structure, not raw nats, carry across

\section{Fatigue and the Cross-Dimension DSD-Onset Gap}
\label{sec:fatigue}

Before sweeping $q$ we confirm the metric is sound here. The $n\approx71$--$73$
divergence of Section~\ref{sec:dimonset} is reference-robust: re-derived from the
stored profiles under three independent references (Li--Nguyen, GSA-zero, and
the reference-free $\frac1m\sum_i|r_i^{\mathrm{BKZ}}-r_i^{\mathrm{SD}}|$), it
remains large under all three ($32.7$ nats reference-free at $n=73$ vs $0.03$ at
$n=67$). The \emph{signed} advantage, by contrast, is a reference artifact on
NTRU (Section~\ref{sec:bg-obs}); accordingly everything below reports the
reference-free DSD observable, never the signed quantity.

Sweeping $q$ across the fatigue point at fixed $n$, the reference-free
BKZ-vs-SD-BKZ profile divergence peaks near $q\!\approx\!q_\mathrm{fat}$. The
SD-BKZ DSD-onset occurs at progressively \emph{lower} $q$ than the BKZ onset as
$n$ grows---the gap widens with dimension:

\subsection{Convergence dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}
Beyond \emph{whether} SD-BKZ reaches dense-sublattice discovery first, the
per-tour trajectories show \emph{how fast}. Tracking the mean $d(\mathrm{LN})$
each tour at a fixed overstretched modulus, SD-BKZ reaches the converged
$d(\mathrm{LN})$ that BKZ attains only at the end of its run roughly midway
through its own run---at tour $9$ of $18$ ($n=89$), $22$ of $42$ ($n=101$), and
$25$ of $44$ ($n=113$)---and then continues past it (Figure~\ref{fig:crossover});
the tours-to-match grows with $n$. This is a timing observable measured against
BKZ's own converged value; the signed end-of-run separation is the
reference-sensitive advantage and is not reported as a result
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-obs}).

\section{Cross-Engine: SD-BKZ on a Sieve Oracle}
\label{sec:xengine}

The G6K engine seam and the self-dual pump-and-jump construction are described
in Section~\ref{sec:setup-engines}; recall that the construction was validated
against fplll's \texttt{SD\_VARIANT} by matching the per-tour GS-slope-flattening
signature ($-0.00039$ vs $-0.00040$). This section reports four things: a
cross-engine soundness check (Table~\ref{tab:xeng-sound}), an honest DSD-onset
\emph{null} at $n=89$, the gap reopening at $n\ge101$ (Table~\ref{tab:xengdim}),
and---on a separate axis---a sieve-vs-enumeration difference in how aggressively
the sub-fatigue tail is reduced.

\paragraph{RHF blindness is engine-independent but regime-dependent.}
On NTRU at $q=97$, $\beta=40$, the companion paper's thesis reproduces on the
sieve: at $n=101/113$ the stored Hermite-factor values of the G6K BKZ and
SD-BKZ bases are bit-identical seed-for-seed, and on fplll the BKZ-vs-SD-BKZ
Hermite factors hold to $\le2\times10^{-6}$ across $20$ seeds at each of
$n=67/101/113$ (at $n=67$ to $<2\times10^{-15}$), while
$d(\mathrm{LN})$ differs. The effect is regime-dependent: at $n=89$---the
cell containing the short-vector events---the values diverge broadly on every
seed, fired or not. (Those short-vector firings are a looser screen than DSD
collapse, so the same $n=89$ is a DSD-onset \emph{null} below---different
thresholds, not a contradiction.) This is consistent with
RHF-equality reported under a non-exact (approximate-enumeration)
oracle~\cite{ABLR21,Rowell2024}; the present work measures the structure RHF misses.

\paragraph{Cross-engine validation of the construction.}
A dimension-resolved fplll-vs-G6K comparison at $q=97$ ($n=89/101/113$, 20
seeds each, $\beta=40$, 50 tours) confirms the self-dual G6K construction is
sound (Table~\ref{tab:xeng-sound}). A run ``fires'' when its first GS
log-norm drops below $3.5$---a deliberately loose short-vector screen used
only for this agreement check, never as a DSD flag. At $n=101/113$ neither
engine fires (the Jaccard $1.00$ is vacuous agreement on the empty set). At
$n=89$ fplll's single firing (seed 19) is corroborated by G6K with the
identical first GS log-norm $2.332$, and G6K additionally fires on one seed
(17) that the deterministic enumeration misses---consistent with a randomised
oracle finding a genuinely short vector. fplll's exact oracle never fires
where the sieve does not, so the $n=89$ short-vector events are real but
sparse, not an artifact of the approximate oracle.

\paragraph{At a matched $\beta=40$ the two engines agree: no DSD-onset gap.}
We run the \emph{same} grid ($n=89$, $\beta=40$, $50$ tours, 15 moduli
$q\in[97,211]$ including a dense onset window $q\in\{191,193,197,199\}$, 100
seeds per cell) on both the fplll enumeration engine and the G6K sieve, under
the \emph{proper} two-part DSD criterion of Section~\ref{sec:bg-obs} (profile
collapse, $\#\{i:\log\|\mathbf{b}_i^\ast\|<2.888\}\le n+1$ at $n=89$, \emph{and}
$\min_i\log\|\mathbf{b}_i^\ast\|>1.5$). The two-part form matters. The looser
$b_1>1.5$ test alone over-fires on uniformly-reduced sub-fatigue bases, where
the tail is cleared but the profile has not collapsed onto the sublattice:
read as a DSD flag, it converts the $q=137$ clearing counts of
Table~\ref{tab:g6konset} ($38$ vs $12$ enum, $61$ vs $23$ sieve) into a
spurious ``matched cross-engine DSD gap''---a claim an earlier draft of this
report made, and which the two-part criterion refutes (the audit record is
\texttt{results/validation/dsd\_criterion\_sensitivity.json}).

Under the proper criterion the cross-engine picture is a clean \emph{null}: at $q=137$
neither SD-BKZ nor BKZ fires DSD on \emph{either} engine, and the onset is
pinned by the dense window at 100 seeds per cell---fplll SD $193.7$ / BKZ
$193.8$, G6K SD $193.1$ / BKZ $194.0$. The fplll gap is $+0.16$ modulus units
(bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.04, +0.46]$, spans zero) and the G6K gap is
$+0.82$ modulus units ($[+0.28, +2.19]$, well below the $q$-grid
resolution but a slight SD$<$BKZ tendency on the sieve): no DSD-onset
gap above grid resolution on either oracle
(Table~\ref{tab:g6konset}). The DSD rate rises smoothly through the window
($37\%$ at $q=191$ to $85\%$ at $q=199$, $100\%$ at $q=211$, near-identical
curves for both variants and both engines), so the null is
measurement-resolved, not grid-limited: densifying from $N=40$ to $N=100$
moved every onset by less than one modulus unit.

\paragraph{Where the tail clearing lands: a cross-engine per-position view.}
The separate min-GS-clearing event of Table~\ref{tab:g6konset} (SD$>$BKZ on both
engines at $q=137$) is a redistribution of profile mass, not a uniform
improvement. Figure~\ref{fig:perpos} plots the per-position difference
$\log\|\mathbf{b}_i^\ast\|_{\mathrm{SD}}-\log\|\mathbf{b}_i^\ast\|_{\mathrm{BKZ}}$
over the $q$-sweep: in the pre-onset band ($q\in[127,157]$) self-dual reduction
pulls mass head-down and pushes it tail-up, concentrated in the last few GS
vectors, and is flat in the onset regime ($q\ge181$). This is largely a
corollary of the flatter SD profile under determinant conservation---a fit to
pure slope-flattening explains ${\approx}96\%$ of the per-position signal
($R^2=0.957$)---so we read it as a \emph{visualisation} of the
cross-engine-agreeing slope flattening rather than an independent mechanism.
The pattern holds on both the fplll enumeration and the G6K sieve (the sieve
slightly larger in magnitude), placing the agreement at the level of the
reduction's structural effect, not the oracle.

What the matched-$\beta$ comparison \emph{does} surface is a structural difference
between the oracles themselves. The min-GS-clearing event---an aggressively reduced tail
($\min_i\log\|\mathbf{b}_i^\ast\|>1.5$, short of full DSD collapse)---fires more
often under SD-BKZ than BKZ on both engines ($38$ vs $12$ on fplll, $61$ vs $23$
on G6K at $q=137$, 100 seeds), and under SD-BKZ the G6K sieve leaves the
shortest GS vector far longer than fplll enumeration on identical instances:
median log-norm $1.844$ (sieve) vs $0.255$ (enum), $+1.59$ nats
${\approx}4.9\times$ in vector norm (under plain BKZ the difference is small:
$0.140$ vs $0.104$). The sieve thus reduces the sub-fatigue tail more
\emph{broadly}---more of its tail vectors clear the floor ($61$ vs fplll's $38$
at $q=137$)---yet leaves its single shortest vector \emph{longer}: broad, not
deep. This is a genuine
cross-engine observation---both engines start from the byte-identical primal
basis ($100/100$ seeds) with the determinant conserved (median drift
${\sim}10^{-6}$).

The reference-free observable remains essential on these cells: where the
min-GS-clearing event fires, the \emph{signed} $d(\mathrm{LN})$ advantage is
large and negative on both engines, because an aggressively reduced $q$-ary tail
is far from the LWE-derived $d(\mathrm{LN})$ reference. Report the unsigned
profile structure, never the signed advantage.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Three observations bear on how SD-BKZ is modelled in NTRU security estimation.
First, the SD-BKZ advantage is \emph{not} a smooth function of dimension: the
sharp onset spike at $n\approx71$--$73$ (Section~\ref{sec:dimonset}), exceeding
the plateau, means a security estimate interpolated from a coarse $n$-grid can
miss where self-dual reduction is most effective. Second, the quantity that
captures the SD-vs-BKZ difference on NTRU is reference-free: the signed
$d(\mathrm{LN})$ advantage flips sign under the reference and must not be
reported (Section~\ref{sec:bg-obs}); the defensible observables are the unsigned
profile divergence and the DSD flag. Third, and most consequential, the
DSD-onset \emph{gap}---SD-BKZ reaching dense-sublattice discovery at lower $q$
than BKZ---grows with dimension at $\beta=20$ (Section~\ref{sec:fatigue}). At
matched $\beta=40$ the enumeration and sieving oracles agree at $n=89$
(within $1$ modulus unit at 100 seeds/cell, Section~\ref{sec:xengine});
at $n=101$ the gap reopens on both oracles ($9\%$ fplll, $7\%$ G6K),
establishing the cross-engine claim at that dimension.
Where the two oracles differ at $\beta=40$ is the aggressiveness of sub-fatigue
tail reduction---the G6K sieve clears the shortest GS vector markedly more than
fplll enumeration on identical instances. A model that treats SD-BKZ and BKZ as
RHF-equivalent (as the Root Hermite Factor, blind here, would suggest) misses
that at $\beta=20$ self-dual reduction reaches dense-sublattice structure at a
lower modulus than BKZ on identical bases. In estimator terms: scoring the two
as RHF-equal puts their NTRU DSD onset at the same modulus, whereas SD-BKZ
reaches it measurably lower---a direction a conservative defender should not
assume away, though we do not quantify its bit-level impact here.

These findings sit alongside two lines of work. The $\beta=40$ regime and the
Z-shaped $q$-ary profile geometry match the small-modulus forgery setting of
Ducas--Espitau--Postlethwaite~\cite{DEP2023}, where the same profile structure
is the attack surface. And the move away from an RHF-centric view toward
sublattice density echoes recursive lattice reduction~\cite{RecursiveReduction2023},
which abandons the Hermite-factor framing for a Rankin/dense-sublattice one---the
same direction our reference-free observable points. We do not benchmark
against speed-oriented reduction tools such as \textsf{flatter}~\cite{RyanHeninger2023}:
the object of study is basis \emph{structure} at a fixed reduction effort
(profile convergence, DSD onset), not attack wall-clock, so the relevant
comparison axis is oracle type (enumeration vs sieving), which
Section~\ref{sec:xengine} covers.

We are careful about scope. The dimension-onset spike is shown at $\beta=20$,
$q=97$; the cross-engine comparison at $\beta=40$, $n=89$. The signed $d(\mathrm{LN})$
is reported only on the $q=97$ dimension sweep (Section~\ref{sec:dimonset}),
where it is reference-robust---verified under three independent references at the
onset (Section~\ref{sec:fatigue})---and never in the $q$-onset analysis, where it
is a reference artifact; and the cross-engine comparison at $\beta=40$,
$n=89$ is an honest null on the DSD-onset (a regime constraint, not a
confirmation), its positive content being the sieve-vs-enum tail-reduction
difference; the matched-$\beta=40$ gap reopens at $n=101$ on both engines ($9\%$ fplll,
$7\%$ G6K, Section~\ref{sec:xengine}), each engine's SD-BKZ fire set a
near-superset of its BKZ set. The criterion-sensitivity episode of Section~\ref{sec:xengine}
generalises: a binary structural flag should test the structure (here, profile
collapse onto the sublattice), not a single order statistic that aggressive
reduction can satisfy without the structure being present. And $n=127$ is
excluded from the onset trend
(Section~\ref{sec:fatigue}): the fplll GSO cancellation
~\cite[\S8]{Paper1} contaminated those cells, and the circulant crack
there is off-grid---it is a validation target
(Appendix~\ref{sec:kahan}), not a trend point.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}
Beyond the per-seed JSON / SHA-256 / named-campaign apparatus of
Section~\ref{sec:setup-repro}: the cross-engine soundness records and DSD-onset
tables are committed under \texttt{results/validation/} with their own
reproduction commands, and the determinism and self-dual-semantics decisions are
recorded in ADRs~005--008. Each figure has one module under
\texttt{analysis/plots/}, all regenerated by \texttt{analysis/paper2\_figures.py}:
Figures~\ref{fig:dimonset} and~\ref{fig:violins} from the per-seed
\texttt{advantage} field,
Figures~\ref{fig:perpos} and~\ref{fig:perposdims} from the per-seed GS-log-norm
profiles,
Figures~\ref{fig:phase}, \ref{fig:sigmoids}, \ref{fig:phasemap}
and~\ref{fig:xengonset} from the two-part DSD criterion
(\verb|extract_dsd_onset.py|, so figures and onset tables cannot disagree), and
Figure~\ref{fig:crossover} from the per-seed per-tour
\texttt{*\_dln\_per\_tour} arrays. The companion
paper's SHA baselines are preserved: the NTRU generator and G6K engine were added
without altering the seed JSON schema. In total the study comprises 8{,}144 matched
BKZ / SD-BKZ reductions (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}), about 5{,}840 core-hours of
reduction across both oracles; every cell regenerates from its named campaign.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
On NTRU, the difference between BKZ and self-dual BKZ that the Root Hermite
Factor cannot see is real, reference-free, and consequential: SD-BKZ reaches
dense-sublattice discovery at a lower modulus than BKZ, with a gap that grows
with dimension at $\beta=20$. At a matched $\beta=40$ the enumeration and sieving
oracles agree on the onset at $n=89$---there they differ instead in how
aggressively they reduce the sub-fatigue tail, the sieve markedly more
than enumeration. At $n=101$ the gap reopens on both engines---$9\%$ on fplll
(SD $257.3$ / BKZ $280.7$) and $7\%$ on G6K (SD $249.9$ / BKZ $268.3$,
Section~\ref{sec:xengine}); the cross-engine gap holds. The
companion paper argued the RHF is blind on LWE; this report shows the blindness
persists on NTRU and on a sieve. That blindness is dimension-dependent and
engine-independent: the apparatus measures structure the RHF misses, across
two oracles. The apparatus---a
determinism-gated, containerised, two-engine benchmark with per-seed evidence---is
released so the measurements can be reproduced and extended.

\section{Re-Validating the fplll GSO Fix on a New Modulus Family}
\label{sec:kahan}

The companion paper documents a catastrophic-cancellation bug in fplll's
squared-form GSO update at cryptographic moduli, and a Kahan-compensated fix.
We re-validate that fix on a new (NTRU) modulus family: on $q\in\{971,1087,1201\}$
at $n=127$, the five contaminated runs (four BKZ, one SD-BKZ) recover from
the precision-floor clamp ($b_1 = -345.388$) to $-0.160$, $-0.123$, $-0.060$,
$-0.070$, and $-0.113$---inside the never-contaminated control band ($-0.03$
to $-0.43$), within which the patched control runs also remain---and the
patched build passes all 15 fplll regression tests. The patch removes the
\emph{catastrophic} cancellation specifically: 208 transient mid-trajectory
clamps persist at this dimension and precision ($11$ shallower than
$-0.1$; $157$ in $-0.1$ to $-3.5$; $40$ deeper, down to $-173$); raw values are
side-logged while the clamped value enters the stored profile. Separately, five
of the twenty-four patched runs end with $b_1$ at the $-345.388$ floor (one
SD-BKZ, four BKZ): these are not residual contamination but genuine
dense-sublattice cracks---the circulant off-grid at $n=127$, the same crack that
excludes $n=127$ from the trend of Section~\ref{sec:fatigue}---whose collapsed
profiles bottom out at the clamp floor. The fix is thus validated on two modulus
families (the companion paper's $q=3329$ LWE, $38\%\!\to\!0\%$ degeneracy, and
this NTRU set).
