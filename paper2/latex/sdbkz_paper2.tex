\begin{abstract}
% [TODO] Finalise once the Phase-4b cross-engine DSD-onset q-sweep lands.
This technical report extends the self-dual BKZ (SD-BKZ) reduction benchmark
of the companion paper from LWE-Kannan lattices to \emph{NTRU}, and from a
single enumeration engine (fplll) to a second, sieving engine (G6K). We
report three empirical results. First, on circulant NTRU lattices the SD-BKZ
advantage exhibits a sharp \emph{dimension-onset}: tied with BKZ for small
$n$, a high-variance spike around $n\!\approx\!71$--$73$ (mean advantage up to
$+9.7$ nats), then a tight $+1.5$-nat plateau for $n\!\ge\!79$. Second, the
defensible NTRU observable is \emph{reference-free}: signed $d(\mathrm{LN})$
is a reference artifact on NTRU (no canonical fixed point), but the BKZ-vs-SD-BKZ
profile divergence / dense-sublattice-discovery (DSD) onset is robust, and the
SD-vs-BKZ DSD-onset \emph{gap grows with dimension} ($0\%\!\to\!27\%$ over
$n\!=\!67$--$113$) as $q$ crosses the fatigue point. Third, the cross-engine
study confirms the companion paper's thesis from the other side---the Root
Hermite Factor is blind to the SD-BKZ difference while $d(\mathrm{LN})$/DSD
sees it---now on a sieve oracle, and finds the effect to be regime-dependent.
We also re-validate, on a new modulus family, an fplll GSO numerical fix. All
experiments are reproducible via a containerised, determinism-gated benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The companion paper~\cite{Paper1} % [TODO: replace Paper1 with the published key/DOI]
showed, on LWE-Kannan lattices, that the Root Hermite Factor (RHF) is
structurally blind to differences between BKZ and self-dual
BKZ~\cite{EC:MicWal16} that the Li--Nguyen Rankin profile distance
$d(\mathrm{LN})$~\cite{EPRINT:LiNgu20} makes visible. This report asks two
follow-up questions a data paper is well suited to answer: \emph{(i) does the
SD-BKZ phenomenology generalise beyond LWE to NTRU?} and \emph{(ii) does it
reproduce on a fundamentally different reduction oracle---sieving (G6K) rather
than enumeration?}

Our contributions are primarily empirical and infrastructural:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A characterisation of the SD-BKZ advantage on circulant NTRU
        lattices~\cite{DucasWoerden2021}, including a sharp dimension-onset
        transition (Section~\ref{sec:dimonset}).
  \item Evidence that the appropriate NTRU observable is reference-free, and a
        \emph{dense-sublattice-discovery (DSD) onset} whose SD-vs-BKZ gap grows
        with dimension as $q$ approaches the fatigue point
        (Section~\ref{sec:fatigue}).
  \item A cross-engine study (Section~\ref{sec:xengine}): G6K wired as a second
        engine behind a reproducibility-gated seam, a self-dual pump-and-jump
        construction validated against fplll's SD-BKZ, and confirmation that
        RHF-blindness is engine-independent but regime-dependent---consistent
        with independent observations of RHF-equality under a non-exact
        oracle~\cite{Rowell2024}.
  \item An independent re-validation of an fplll GSO catastrophic-cancellation
        fix on a new (NTRU) modulus family (Section~\ref{sec:kahan}).
\end{itemize}

This is a \emph{technical report / data paper}: the emphasis is on the
experimental apparatus, the data, and reproducibility, rather than on new
theory. Closest prior work is Deng and Jia's self-dual BKZ over a sieve
oracle~\cite{DengJia2023}; we differ in measuring the SD-vs-BKZ structural
difference via the reference-free profile observable rather than swapping the
oracle for speed. White~\cite{White26} studies dynamic block-size strategies
within the same Li--Nguyen framework, complementary to the fixed-$\beta$,
$q$-swept regime characterised here.

\section{The SD-BKZ Advantage Has a Dimension-Onset on NTRU}
\label{sec:dimonset}

On circulant NTRU at $q=97$ (standard regime), $\beta=20$, $50$ tours, the
SD-BKZ-vs-BKZ advantage has a \emph{sharp} dimension transition, not a smooth
ramp (20 seeds per $n$):

\begin{table}[tbp]
\centering
\caption{SD-BKZ advantage on NTRU: tied $\to$ onset spike $\to$ plateau.\label{tab:dimonset}}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & mean adv & std & seeds$>0$ \\
\midrule
59--67 & $\sim 0$  & tight & 3--9/20 \\
71     & $+4.35$   & 5.59  & 16/20 \\
73     & $+9.68$   & 5.21  & 19/20 \\
79--89 & $+1.5$    & $\sim 0.2$ & 20/20 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three regimes: tied for $n\!\le\!67$; an onset zone at $n=71$--$73$ where the
mean spikes to $+4$--$10$ nats with ${\sim}25\times$ the plateau variance
(SD-BKZ cracks the dense sublattice on most-but-not-all instances); and a
stable $+1.5$-nat plateau for $n\!\ge\!79$ (std ${\approx}0.2$, $20/20$ wins).
The onset spike exceeds the plateau---non-monotonic.

\label{sec:xengine}

\paragraph{RHF blindness is engine-independent but regime-dependent.}
On NTRU at $q=97$, $\beta=40$, the companion paper's thesis reproduces on the
sieve: in the standard regime the RHF of BKZ and SD-BKZ bases is identical to
machine precision while $d(\mathrm{LN})$ differs. The effect is
regime-dependent---RHF-blindness breaks only on the rare seeds where SD-BKZ
finds a short vector (which RHF reflects). This is consistent with
RHF-equality reported under a non-exact (approximate-enumeration)
oracle~\cite{Rowell2024}; the present work measures the structure RHF misses.

\paragraph{Cross-engine validation of the construction.}
A dimension-resolved fplll-vs-G6K comparison ($n=89/101/113$, 20 seeds each)
confirms the self-dual G6K construction is sound: at $n=101/113$ the two
engines agree perfectly (no spurious sieve firing), and where a short vector
genuinely exists ($n=89$, seed 19) both engines land the identical first
GS log-norm. % [TODO] table: per-n fired counts + Jaccard; the n=89 sparse-but-real result.

\section{Re-Validating the fplll GSO Fix on a New Modulus Family}
\label{sec:kahan}

The companion paper documents a catastrophic-cancellation bug in fplll's
squared-form GSO update at cryptographic moduli, and a Kahan-compensated fix.
We re-validate that fix on a new (NTRU) modulus family: on $q\in\{971,1087,1201\}$
at $n=127$, the four contaminated seeds recover from the precision-floor clamp
($b_1 = -345.388$) into the control band (${\approx}-0.1$); the patched build
passes all 15 fplll regression tests. The fix is thus validated on two modulus
families (the companion paper's $q=3329$ LWE, $38\%\!\to\!0\%$ degeneracy, and
this NTRU set).
